Hey guys!! I made it to Provo this week, so I've finally started in-person MTC. I met my district and my companions (who are Sister Nelson for those who didn't read my first email). My district is a lot of fun! We spend a decent amount of time outside of class together-- we eat together and do some activities together.

On Sunday, not much happened. I had more free time than I did on Saturday, which was a welcome break.

On Monday, I had classes and then I spent all night packing. 

On Tuesday, I flew out to provo and got to the MTC a bit before lunch. Because I was early and my companions weren't going to show up until the next day, I got to spend time with some other Sisters. They have been at the MTC almost nine weeks-- they're speaking Mandrin on their mission, so they have a longer amount of time in the MTC. Their names are Sisters Kent, Kim and Griffyn, and they are a lot of fun to hang around with. I've seen them occasionally at lunch and around campus since Tuesday, and it's always fun to say hi. We also had a devotional from Elder and Sister Basset for the Tuesday General Authority devotional. I also met Sister Woodford-- the Sister I shared a room with Tuesday night-- and she's a lot of fun to be around.

Wednesday was my first official day at the MTC. No one in my district came early, so I met them all that afternoon. The first six hours of the day consisted of sitting in a room while a bunch of people talked at us. I zoned out at the first 45 minutes and had to take Sister Woodford out of the room to grab something to do from my luggage. It was obvious that they just wanted us early arrivals out of the way before our district meeting times. It was really boring-- they got the most monochrome talkers they had access to in their senior MTC volunteer arsenal. They spoke off of a Papyrus font themed slideshow, which was entertaining to the extent that they obviously chose the first powerpoint theme they saw.

FINALLY at 1:30 they allowed us to take our luggage to our resident halls and unpack while waiting for our district to show up. After the girls in the district that I'm sharing a room with showed up, we went to class where we met the rest of our district.

On Thursday we had a class and met the branch presidency and the president's wife is Sister Bonnie Oscarson-- the General Young Womens President (a church leader for the youth) from 2013-2018. She has seven general conference talks, so if you are interested, you can find them on Gospel Library, look them up on Google,or you can message me and I can copy and paste them and send them to you via email.

We went to the temple on Friday! We took a bus ride to the Mt. Tempanogos temple in American Fort Utah. We did baptisms and initiatories for the dead. The initiatory ordinace is something we do to prepare ourselves for the endowment ceremony, which is where we make additional covenants with God to become better people and love and serve those around us. If you want to learn more about what happens in the temple, please reach out to me or other missionaries in your area :)

There's not much to do at the MTC when you don't have classes or places to be, so today has been a bit long. I did laundry and wrote a bunch of emails to various people, along with the SUUUPER long one you're reading right now (thanks for reading this far to those who did). Our district went to the activity fields and played sand volleyball for about an hour, then we went to a room with three pianos where I played and wrote my email while my companions talked to family and listened to conference talks.

Spiritual thought:
In one of our classes, we talked about how we can use the Book of Mormon to answer questions we have, and we were invited to choose a couple and do that during class. One of my questions was how I can feel God's forgiveness. In Alma 36:17-21, it talks about how we need to remember Jesus Christ's love for us and that He is always on our side. What I got from this was that Jesus will help us in our endeavors to repent and become more like Him, and if we feel like we aren't forgiven if we ask in prayer.

Live long and prosper,
Sister Christison
